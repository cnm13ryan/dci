### 3b. Durable storage and the restore path

v13 mirrored committed batches to the durable root and had no way back. A Colab
disconnect therefore cost the whole local cache.

v14 adds `restore_from_drive`, which runs at startup and before every
generation stage. It reads the durable mirror, and for each committed batch
verifies the payload hash, the execution fingerprint, the tokenizer and
chat-template hashes, and membership of the current batch plan, before copying
it into the local fast cache. Anything that fails is quarantined with a reason
rather than deleted or silently ignored.

The consequence is that a disconnect loses at most the batch that was in flight
when it happened. Gate 0 proves this by generating committed batches, deleting
the local cache, restoring, and asserting that no completed row is regenerated.

Cross-filesystem replacement is not atomic on a mounted Drive, so writes go to a
temporary file in the destination directory, are renamed within that
filesystem, and are then read back and hash-verified.
