## **Guidelines for a Good Abstract**

Synthesizing [Neel Nanda's advice on writing ML papers](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/eJGptPbbFPZGLpjsp/highly-opinionated-advice-on-how-to-write-ml-papers) with the [ICML best practices](https://icml.cc/Conferences/2022/BestPractices), a strong abstract orients a cold reader by identifying the paper's genre, contribution, evidence, and stakes.

**Core function.** An abstract is a *cold-start orientation tool*. It tells readers what the paper is about, what is new, why the claim is credible, and why the result matters.

## Canonical Structure

1. **Sentence 1 — Field:** State an uncontroversial fact that situates the paper in its subfield.
2. **Sentence 2 — Gap:** Identify the need, problem, unknown, or gap motivating the work.
3. **Sentence 3 — Contribution:** State the headline contribution clearly and concisely.
4. **Optional Sentence 4 — Clarification:** Define essential terms or add necessary technical detail.
5. **Next Few Sentences — Evidence:** Present one supporting claim or result per sentence, with concrete numbers where possible.
6. **Final 1–2 Sentences — Impact:** Explain why the contribution matters, its implications, and the strength of the evidence.

## Quality Criteria

* **Write for a cold-start expert reader.** Provide enough context for the abstract to stand alone.
* **State 1–3 coherent claims.** Compress to a single thread.
* **Make claims explicit and concrete.** Show how the evidence supports each claim.
* **Ground each claim in concrete numbers or metrics.**
* **Use plain language.** Define jargon required for precision.
* **Position the contribution against prior work** so the novelty is legible.
* **Match confidence language to evidence.** Signal whether the work is preliminary, compelling, best-practice-establishing, or misconception-correcting.
* **Make the title contribution-focused and memorable.**
* **State limitations honestly.** Calibrate conclusions to the evidence.
