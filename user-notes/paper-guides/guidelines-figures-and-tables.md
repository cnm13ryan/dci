## **Guidelines for Good Figures and Tables**

Synthesizing [Neel Nanda's advice on writing ML papers](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/eJGptPbbFPZGLpjsp/highly-opinionated-advice-on-how-to-write-ml-papers) with the [ICML best practices](https://icml.cc/Conferences/2022/BestPractices), a strong figure or table should carry argumentative weight, communicate a specific claim, and remain comprehensible to a skimming reader.

**Core function.** A figure or table is *visual evidence in service of a specific claim*: it answers an implicit question, supports that answer with a form fit to the data, and conveys its takeaway to a reader who sees only the visual and its caption.

**Canonical structure**

1. **Purpose:** State the figure/table's type (results, schematic, table, illustrative) and the specific claim it supports. Identify the implicit question it answers.
2. **Necessity:** Reserve figures for content that benefits from visual form. Each figure should do work that requires visual representation.
3. **Reference and Placement:** Cite the figure or table in the prose at the point where its supporting claim is made.
4. **Active Caption:** Write a caption that states the takeaway, communicating the figure's main point alongside its subject.
5. **Stand-Alone Comprehensibility:** The figure together with its caption and legend should convey what is shown and why it matters, sufficient for a reader who skips the prose.
6. **Labels and Visual Elements:** Make every axis, row, column, label, and visual element either self-evident or explained in the caption or prose.
7. **Form Fits Claim:** Choose the visual or tabular form that best fits the claim and directs attention to what matters. The impression conveyed should remain stable across reasonable alternative cuts, groupings, baselines, or chart types.
8. **Visual Integrity:** Use axes, scales, color semantics, and hierarchy that represent the data faithfully and emphasize what matters.
9. **Argumentative Coherence:** Across the paper, ensure figures and tables collectively strengthen the central argument.

**Quality criteria**

* **Pair each figure with a specific claim.** Every figure or table should support a clearly identifiable argument.
* **Write active, takeaway-oriented captions.** A reader should learn the point from the caption alone.
* **Ensure stand-alone comprehensibility.** A skimming reader should grasp what is shown and why it matters from the figure, caption, and legend together.
* **Cite at the point of claim.** Reference figures and tables in the prose where the supporting argument is made.
* **Reserve figures for visual work.** Use figures when content requires visual representation to be understood.
* **For results figures:** include relevant baselines, controls, and error bars; match the scope of plotted data to the scope of the claim; report the full range of conditions tested.
* **For tables:** include all relevant rows and columns, use comparable categories, and maintain consistent units.
* **For schematics:** make assumptions explicit and clarify the underlying mechanism.
* **Maintain visual integrity.** Use axes and scales that represent the data faithfully, color semantics that carry meaning, and hierarchy that emphasizes what matters.
* **Explain every label.** Each axis, row, column, and visual element should be defined in the caption, legend, or prose.
* **Align caption, prose, and figure.** The takeaway in the caption, the supporting prose, and the visual should all communicate the same claim.
* **State the argument explicitly in the prose.** Use the figure to support the written claim, with the text carrying the argument.
* **Audit the paper as a whole.** Across all figures and tables, check that visual types vary as the content requires, captions communicate takeaways, baselines are present where expected, and every label is explained.
