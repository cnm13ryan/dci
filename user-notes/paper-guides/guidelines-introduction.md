## **Guidelines for a Good Introduction**

Synthesizing [Neel Nanda's advice on writing ML papers](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/eJGptPbbFPZGLpjsp/highly-opinionated-advice-on-how-to-write-ml-papers) with the [ICML best practices](https://icml.cc/Conferences/2022/BestPractices), a strong introduction expands the abstract into a clear, self-contained narrative.

**Core function.** The Introduction is an *extended abstract*. It situates the work, motivates the problem, states the main claims, previews the evidence, and explains the stakes before the technical body begins. Its goal is to help skimming readers understand the contribution and help engaged readers decide to continue.

## Canonical Structure

1. **Paragraph 1 — Context:** Introduce the topic, motivating question, and significance. Cite key work to establish the field, show why the problem matters, and situate prior attempts. End by previewing how the contribution addresses the question.
2. **Paragraph 2 — Technical Background:** Summarize the concepts, methods, and prior approaches the paper builds on. Identify what has been tried, what remains inadequate, and where the gap lies.
3. **Paragraph 3 — Key Claim:** State the main claim precisely, including the technical nuance omitted from the abstract. Define essential terminology needed to understand the contribution.
4. **Paragraph 3.5 or 4 — Key Evidence:** Summarize the strongest evidence for the main claim, including headline experiments and central results. The evidence may share Paragraph 3 or stand alone, depending on length.
5. **Optional Additional Paragraphs — Secondary Claims:** Repeat the claim → evidence pattern for each additional claim, connecting it to the main contribution.
6. **Impact Paragraph:** Explain the paper's implications, who should act on them, and what should change. Calibrate strength using language such as preliminary, compelling, best-practice-establishing, or misconception-correcting.
7. **Contributions List:** End with concise bullet points pairing each key claim with its strongest evidence.

## Quality Criteria

* **Separate the existing gap from the contribution.** Make clear where prior work ends and the paper begins.
* **Pair each claim with a brief explanation of its evidence.**
* **Clarify novelty.** State what is new, what builds on prior work, and what extends existing methods.
* **Use citations purposefully.** Each citation supports a specific claim, comparison, or attribution.
* **Write a self-contained introduction.** Give expert readers enough context to understand the paper from the introduction alone, and point others to useful background sources.
* **Match the strength of each claim to the strength of the evidence.**
* **Use plain, precise language.**
* **Repeat the core idea strategically.** Restate the contribution through motivation, claim, evidence, and impact so it sticks.
* **Foreshadow the paper's structure.** Promise only what the body delivers.
* **Support skimming and random access.** Make the problem, notation, claims, and contributions easy to extract.
* **Calibrate confidence.** Use language appropriate to the evidence: preliminary, narrow, systematic, existence-proof, compelling, or definitive.
* **Polish the introduction carefully.** Treat it as comparable in importance to the abstract, figures, and main results.

## Editorial Note

The 7-step structure is a starting template. Paragraphs 3 and 3.5 may merge. Secondary-claim paragraphs may use shorter or longer treatments depending on the evidence.
