## **Guidelines for a Good Background / Preliminary Section**

Synthesizing [Neel Nanda's advice on writing ML papers](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/eJGptPbbFPZGLpjsp/highly-opinionated-advice-on-how-to-write-ml-papers) with the [ICML best practices](https://icml.cc/Conferences/2022/BestPractices), a strong Background/Preliminaries section makes the paper's technical setting clear, self-contained, and easy to follow.

**Core function.** The Background section orients readers to the technical context of the paper. It defines the necessary terminology, notation, problem setting, standard methods, and relevant prior work so an expert reader can understand the rest of the paper without hidden assumptions.

## Core Moves

1. **Problem Setup:** Define the problem or formal setting. State assumptions explicitly. Keep the setup separate from the paper's contributions.
2. **Notation:** Introduce notation in a skimmable, easy-to-reference format.
3. **Standard Methods and Concepts:** Define each core concept or technique the paper builds on. For each, give the name, formal definition, intuition, and canonical citation.
4. **Place Novel Material Outside Background:** Put new definitions, methods, or contributions in a clearly separate section so prior work and contributions remain distinguishable.
5. **Point to Deeper Resources:** Cite surveys, tutorials, or review articles for readers needing more background.

## Quality Criteria

* **Make the section self-contained for expert readers.** Provide enough background, with citations, for an expert to follow the paper.
* **Calibrate definitions to the audience.** Define specialized, recent, or paper-critical terms. Skip concepts universally known to the target audience.
* **State assumptions explicitly.**
* **Cite external results precisely.** Reference theorem numbers, sections, pages, or equations where possible.
* **Keep the section minimal.** Include only what readers need to understand the paper.
* **Use plain, precise language.** Reach for jargon when it improves accuracy.
* **Support skimming.** Use clear headings, bolded terms, and easy-to-find definitions.
* **Keep motivation in the Introduction.** The Background covers what readers need to know. The Introduction covers why they should care.
* **Structure paragraphs deliberately.** Begin each paragraph with its purpose and end by connecting it to the paper.

## Editorial Note

The five core moves are the structural elements both sources mandate. Stylistic elements such as opening signposts and closing transitions are optional polish.
