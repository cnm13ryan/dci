# TITLES

**Deferred on review. Do not finalise a title until the literature search is
done** (`GAPS.md` CITE-02 **and CITE-03**): a title has to name the contribution,
and what the contribution *is* depends on both checks, not one. If C2's attrition
account is novel and C1's estimand is not, the title names the identification
account; if C1's directional estimand is novel and C2's account is not, it names
the estimand; if both are, the paper has two and the title picks one; if neither
is, there may be no paper here at all (`ITERATION.md` Q8). **The genre is
unsettled for the same reason** — whether this reads as a methods paper, a
negative result, or an empirical one is downstream of the same two citations.
The three candidates below stay on the table with their problems recorded.

The guidelines library carries no title guideline, so the criteria below are
stated rather than assumed.

## Criteria applied

Drawn from the two places the guidelines do speak to titles —
`guidelines-narrative-and-process.md` ("make the title contribution-focused and
memorable") and `guidelines-abstract.md` (same line, under Quality Criteria) —
plus three constraints this particular paper imposes.

1. **Contribution-focused.** The title names what the paper contributes, not
   what it studied. **What that is cannot be fixed yet** — it is CITE-02 and
   CITE-03 that decide whether the contribution is the identification account
   (C2), the directional estimand (C1), or both. Apply this criterion once the
   search resolves; until then it selects nothing. In particular, do not assume
   the paper is a methods-and-negative-result paper whose contribution is the
   identification requirement — that was the presupposition this deferral
   exists to suspend.
2. **Calibrated.** The title must not assert the thing the study failed to
   identify. Any title containing "interference" as a measured phenomenon
   overclaims. This is the binding constraint here.
3. **Memorable and quotable.** It survives being said aloud in a hallway and
   read in a citation list.
4. **Legible cold.** A reader who knows instruction-following but not this
   programme should get the subject from the title alone. "DCI", "v4r4",
   `Delta_R` and "companion" are internal vocabulary and do not appear.
5. **Honest about genre.** A reader should be able to tell it is a negative and
   methodological result before reading the abstract, so nobody arrives
   expecting an effect size.
6. **Length.** Under about twelve words. A colon is acceptable when the
   left-hand side is the memorable half and the right-hand side carries the
   calibration.

## T1 — *not* the current pick; see the review note below

> **What It Takes to Measure Instruction Interference, and Why We Could Not**

- **Criterion 1:** names the contribution — the requirement — first.
- **Criterion 2:** "why we could not" forecloses the overclaim in the title
  itself, which is the strongest possible placement for it.
- **Criterion 3:** the two-clause turn is the memorable part, and the second
  clause is the one people repeat.
- **Criterion 5:** genre is unambiguous from the title alone.
- **Risk:** the first person is unusual for the venue and reads as informal to
  some reviewers. It also anonymises cleanly, which not every first-person title
  does.

## T2

> **Matched Controls for Composed Instructions: Four Filters and a Failed Design**

- **Criterion 1:** "matched controls" is the contribution; "four filters" is the
  reusable object a reader takes away.
- **Criterion 2:** "failed design" is calibrated and specific — the design
  failed, not the measurement apparatus.
- **Criterion 4:** "matched controls" and "composed instructions" are both
  field-legible without this programme's vocabulary.
- **Risk:** "four filters" invites a reader to expect a taxonomy paper, and the
  number will look arbitrary until §4. Also slightly long.

## T3

> **A Preregistered Instruction-Interference Study That Did Not Identify Its Estimand**

- **Criterion 2:** the most calibrated of the three; "did not identify its
  estimand" is exactly what happened and uses the term the paper defines.
- **Criterion 5:** genre is unmistakable.
- **Criterion 1:** weakest on this criterion — it describes the study rather
  than the contribution, and a reader could conclude there is nothing to take
  away.
- **Risk:** "estimand" is a term some of the audience will not carry, and the
  title reads as an apology rather than a result. Use only if reviewers push
  back on T1 as insufficiently plain.

## Rejected, and why — recorded so they are not re-proposed

- *"Directional Constraint Interference in Composed Instructions"* — violates
  criterion 2 outright. It asserts the phenomenon the study could not identify.
- *"Composition Costs Delivery: Evidence from 49,152 Preregistered
  Generations"* — leads with C1, which is the secondary claim, and the row count
  is impressive rather than informative.
- *"DCI-1: A Preregistered Study of Instruction Interference"* — internal
  vocabulary, no contribution, no calibration.
- *"When Benchmarks Cannot Control for Difficulty"* — good on criteria 1 and 3,
  but drops instructions and composition entirely, so a reader cannot tell what
  is being benchmarked.

**Review note, 2026-08-24.** T1 and T2 both overreach in the same way. *"What
It Takes to Measure Instruction Interference"* asserts general necessary
conditions for measuring the phenomenon; what was established is why **this**
preregistered implementation on **this** candidate library failed. *"Four
Filters"* has the same problem twice over: the four were never homogeneous in
kind, and none is shown to be universally necessary.

**T2 is now unusable as written.** The second review pass removed the
four-filter taxonomy from the package entirely. What C2 carries is registered
gate outcomes plus **two** post-hoc attrition diagnostics that are *not* shown to
be binding causes, plus a structural-class scope restriction that was never a
cause at all. A title that counts filters advertises an object the paper no
longer has.

**T3 is the most epistemically accurate of the three** and is the safe fallback,
at the cost of advertising the study primarily as a failure.

**No title is chosen at this iteration.** If the literature search shows the
identification account is novel, a title in T1's shape becomes defensible once
scoped — for example naming the candidate library rather than the phenomenon. If
it shows prior work has already met the same attrition, the paper's contribution
changes and so does the title.
