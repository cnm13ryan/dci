# DCI-1 v8 — pooled, pooled + top-up, and frame-aware pre-screening compared on the stored evidence

**Status.** Deliverable 4 of the frame-aware pre-screen redesign
(2026-09-03). The numbers are in `prescreen_feasibility_tables.md` and
`summary.json`, generated by `tools/v8_prescreen_counterfactual.py` from
the signed, unprotected pre-freeze records of `dci1v8-pilot-r9` (qwen3-4b),
`-r10` (qwen3-4b-instruct-2507), `-r12` (gemma-3n-e2b-it), `-r13`
(gemma-3n-e4b-it) and the pre-freeze `LOCAL_TOPUP` record of
`dci1v8-confirm-r2`; one row per model × frame × candidate rung is in
`prescreen_counterfactual.csv` (5,960 rows). Zero generation; no protected
stage opened; the replay's restriction of each frame's table to the full
calibrated set reproduces the signed Gate-3 record on all 14 supported
frames (`summary.json → models.*.self_check`), and the legacy replay
reproduces every signed forwarded set.

## The question

> Does frame-aware screening recover materially better downstream
> candidate sets, rather than merely selecting different candidates
> because the screen estimates are noisy?

On the stored evidence — six pre-screen topics per frame, one draw — the
answer is **no, and the reason is measured: the estimates are noisy at
that sample size, whatever the frame.** The comparison is nevertheless
asymmetric in a way that has to be kept in view: the legacy set *is* the
calibrated set, so its recall on the calibrated rungs is 1 by
construction, and every rung a frame-aware policy would forward beyond it
has no verdict anywhere in the study (`NOT_IDENTIFIABLE`).

* On six topics per frame the frame-aware subset loses 15–21 % of the
  LOCAL-valid and 40 % of the SPECTRUM-valid calibrated rungs; it also
  drops half of the rungs that calibrated as neither (10 vs 23 per frame),
  and forwards 13–15 rungs per frame that were never calibrated.
* The spectrum arm is robust under every policy (coverage identified on
  13 of 14 frames throughout); the LOCAL arm is not: `LOCAL_SUPPORTED`
  frames fall from 9 (legacy) to 8 (proxy) and 6 (Gate-2 target), each
  loss being one family short of §13.15's five on the restricted table.
* The top-up fired on one frame of one model (qwen3-4b f2, one rung) and
  moved nothing.

**Stability under topic resampling** (mean Jaccard of the round-0
forwarded set with its resample, 20 resamples):

| model | frame-aware, 6 topics per frame | pooled, 24 topics | pooled, a random 6 of the 24 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| qwen3-4b | 0.49 | 0.72 | 0.49 |
| qwen3-4b-instruct-2507 | 0.58 | 0.64 | 0.54 |
| gemma-3n-e2b-it | 0.50 | 0.63 | 0.54 |
| gemma-3n-e4b-it | 0.48 | 0.66 | 0.50 |

The third column isolates the topic count: the pooled rule on six of its
own topics is as unstable as a frame's six. **The instability is the
sample size, not the frame** — and even the pooled rule at 24 topics keeps
only about two thirds of its set under a resample of its own topics.

**The target.** The frame's screen-proxy ι_A(f) at six topics (+ batches)
drifts from Gate 2's ι_A(f) by −0.17 … +0.08 and has a posterior sd of
0.04–0.15; the pooled screen's ι_A drifts from the same coordinates by
−0.15 … +0.09. The genuine frame effect on ι_A is there (spread across
frames 0.09–0.21 on the calibration coordinate) and neither screen sees it
at this precision. Gate 2's own ι_A(f) has a topic-level SE of 0.02.

**Overlap and compute.** The frame-aware sets overlap across frames by a
mean pairwise Jaccard of 0.32–0.38 (union 91–106 rungs, 5–14 selected by
every frame): on six topics the four frames choose largely different rungs
for noise reasons. Calibration generations: legacy 178–197k per model
(every rung on every frame); frame-aware 140–167k (each rung on the frames
that selected it); the worst case is identical (93 rungs × 4 frames × 768 =
93 × 3,072). A four-pass screen adds ≈ 33k (first pass) + 26–49k (second
pass) per model: about +30 % of a model's Gate-2 generation, ≈ 1–2 h on a
card at the measured throughput of the Gemma-e2b pilot.

## Reading

1. **The pooled screen's advantage on the stored evidence is its
   sample size, nothing else.** Per-frame screening on the same rows is
   worse *because* each frame holds a quarter of the topics; the same rule
   pooled and subsampled to six topics is equally unstable.
2. **The retrospective replay cannot show that a frame-aware screen at the
   pooled screen's budget is better.** No stored root has 24 topics per
   frame; the only stored per-frame evidence is at six. Whether frame
   conditioning at equal budget buys anything is answerable only by a
   fresh pilot that measures the pooled and the frame-aware sets on the
   same calibration rows.
3. **Internal consistency is the case for the redesign, not the replay.**
   The frames' ι_A(f) differ by up to 0.21 on the coordinate every caliper
   is measured against; a pooled screen cannot represent that, and the
   pooled rule was forwarding one set to four different targets. The
   successor's forwarding is a function of frame f's evidence and frame
   f's ι_A(f); the pilot then reports, per frame, the identification each
   policy reaches on identical rows (`GATE_3_REFERENCES.retention_comparison`).
4. **Between the two frame-aware targets the stored evidence is silent**
   (0.79 vs 0.85 recall; 8 vs 6 LOCAL frames, both within six-topic
   noise); the precision argument (proxy drift ±0.05 after the budget
   change vs SE 0.02) picks Gate 2's ι_A(f) as the default, and the pilot
   measures both.
