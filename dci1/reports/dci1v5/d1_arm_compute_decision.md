## 2. Why the spectrum is essential

$\delta_{\text{difficulty}}$ is a member of **F1**, the primary multiplicity
family, and F1's simultaneous interval is a studentised maximum-modulus
critical value taken over its members. Dropping the spectrum does not merely
remove one secondary quantity: it changes F1 from six members to five, so
**every F1 interval — including $D(B{\leftarrow}A)$'s and $\Lambda$'s — becomes
narrower than the one §15 registers.** A study that dropped the spectrum for
cost would report intervals for its primary quantities that a study with it
would not.

It is also §17.6's second lens. A $\Delta_R$ reading and a
$\delta_{\text{difficulty}}$ reading that disagree emit **neither** claim and
record `LOCAL_AND_SPECTRUM_DISAGREE`. Without the spectrum the disagreement
cannot be detected, and the ladder becomes easier to climb — which is the
opposite of what §3.2 registers.

## 3. Why the near-form arm is essential — the new fact

**§34 D18 is closed at `precondition`.** The researcher took the strong
version: the strong L3c conclusion — *no pair-specific excess*, the source
memo's refutation verdict — requires the near-form arm. A same-form Tier-1 pool
alone licenses only the narrower statement, *no excess relative to same-form
exact-count companions*, and `claims.assert_l3c_scope` makes the strong
conclusion **unreachable** without the arm rather than merely unqualified.

So the arithmetic of `four_models_47_cells` has changed. It is not "the same
study, 9 cells cheaper". It is **the study that cannot emit its refutation
verdict**. R1's objection — that v5's core pool is chosen to resemble A closely
enough that $\Delta_R \approx 0$ is a plausible consequence of the *design* —
has exactly one registered mitigation, and that option removes it.

A second, independent reason arrived with U2: $\gamma_{\text{tier}}$'s indicator
does not vary without the near-form controls, so §11.8's secondary fit is not
identified either. Two registered quantities and one claim level, for 9 cells.

## 5. Recommended scientific configuration

> **`four_models_56_cells`** — all five arms, four models, 384 blocks, 8 draws
> per cell. **1,144,896 generations.**

It is the only option that supports the claim set the study is registered to
make. The three cheaper options each give something up, and §6 says what.

**Note on ordering, not on cost.** §6.1's execution order runs the models one
at a time, and a replication not reached is recorded `NOT_EXECUTED` rather than
silently omitted. So four models is a *budget*, not a commitment to finish:
stopping after two leaves two honest replications and two explicit
non-executions, which is a different and better outcome than registering two
models and calling the study complete. **This is why the model count should not
be reduced to buy certainty of completion** — the protocol already handles
running out.

## 6. Lower-cost configurations, and what they give up

### `four_models_47_cells` — saves 119,232 generations (10.4%)
**Gives up the strong L3c.** Under D18's closure this configuration cannot
emit the refutation verdict on any model, cannot estimate
$\Delta_{\text{near}}$, and cannot identify $\gamma_{\text{tier}}$. It also
removes R1's only registered mitigation. **The saving is 10.4% and the cost is
the study's answer to its own third question.** Not recommended at any budget:
if the money is not there for 56 cells it is not there for 47 either, and the
saving is small precisely because the near-form arm is cheap.

### `two_models_56_cells` — saves 572,448 generations (50%)
**Gives up cross-model replication, and only that.** Every estimand, every
claim level and every arm survives; what does not survive is L4-style evidence
about whether a finding holds beyond one model family. Two models means one
gemma and one qwen, which is the minimum that is not a single architecture.
**This is the right economy if one is needed** — it is the only option that
buys a large saving without changing what any single replication can conclude.

### `384_blocks_m4` — saves 344,064 generations (30%)
**Gives up precision, and precision is gated.** Halving the draws per cell
inflates every interval; §16.1's precision preconditions are registered, and a
family that fails one has its claim withheld with `INSUFFICIENT_PRECISION`. So
this option does not buy a cheaper version of the same conclusions — it buys a
study with a higher chance of reaching no conclusion, on a schedule where that
is discovered only after the whole budget is spent. Least attractive of the
three: the saving is smaller than halving the model count and the failure mode
is worse.

**Ranking, if a reduction is forced:** `two_models_56_cells` first,
`384_blocks_m4` a distant second, `four_models_47_cells` last.

**`four_models_56_cells`**, as recommended, on 2026-08-23. The three

`decisions.override_for_testing`, each records what it gives up, and the

What the closure binds, all of it recomputed by `dci1v5.design.budget` from the

four models with their exact 40-character revisions, 384 blocks, $M = 8$,
103,440 calibration and 172,032 confirmatory generations per model, 286,224 per
model in total, **1,144,896** overall.

Two things this packet has **not** done. It has not converted generations into
wall-clock: that depends on the completion cap §12.1 selects per model, on the
pinned vLLM stack, and on TP=1 — none of which is a protocol constant, and a
figure invented here would be quoted later as if it were one. And it has not
recommended reducing the design to fit a budget nobody has stated; if the
budget turns out to be the binding constraint, §6 is the order in which to give
things up, and the *estimand* does not move in any of them except the first.
