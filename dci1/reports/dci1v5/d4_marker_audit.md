## 1. What the audit checked

The researcher's eight criteria, and how each was evidenced:

| # | Criterion | Evidenced by |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | same exact-count operator as A | `FAMILIES[kind].form == exact_count`, asserted at import |
| 2 | deterministic verifier | six tokenisation probes per marker, in the JSON |
| 3 | sufficiently rare naturally | measured $P(\ge 1 \mid \text{unconstrained})$, four models |
| 4 | not semantically equivalent to `however` | declared discourse role + the reading below |
| 5 | not mechanically entailed by the task | the stem states a question and a length; no marker follows from it |
| 6 | realistic joint satisfiability with B | marker length against every rung of the focal grid |
| 7 | varied discourse roles | six roles over eight markers |
| 8 | no lexical feature that changes B | length, morphology, hyphenation, case |

## 2. What the whole vocabulary gets right

**Criterion 1 is structural and holds by construction.** All eight are
`exact_count` families over a marker-occurrence numerator, and the eight
counters are *one function with eight target strings* — the same function, with
`however` substituted, is A's own. That is what makes v5's same-form claim an
identity of implementation rather than a resemblance of prose.

**Criterion 2 holds, measured.** Every marker counts where it appears
(sentence-initial with a comma, quoted, upper-case) and none leaks from a
substring: `although` is not a `though`, `overalls` is not an `overall`,
`enthusiasm` is not a `thus`. Two segmentation behaviours are reported and are
**not** defects: a hyphen splits a word under §9.2, so `semi-thus` counts as a
`thus` and a hyphenated spelling of a marker does not count. A's own counter
behaves identically, which is the point.

**Criterion 6 holds with room to spare.** The registered structural rule is
$\le 7$ letters and B's grid starts at $m = 7$, so **no marker violates B at
any admissible severity** (JSON: `any_marker_violates_b_on_the_grid: false`).
The three seven-letter markers sit *at* the boundary only at $m = 7$; v4r4's
realised operating points were 10, 10, 12, 10, so in practice every marker sat
at least three letters inside the constraint. `overall` and `however` are both
seven letters, so at the tightest admissible rung A and MK8 are matched on the
one lexical feature B reads — which is a property a same-form control should
have, not a coincidence.

**Criterion 7 holds.** Six discourse roles over eight markers — consecutive
(×2), contrastive (×2), emphatic, exemplifying, substitutive, summative. This
is not eight near-synonyms, and §13.8's requirement that the core set span at
least two roles is comfortably reachable.

**Criterion 5 holds.** The stem asks a general-knowledge question and states a
length. No connective is entailed by it.

## 3. The finding that applies to the vocabulary as a whole

Four markers are measured at **exactly 0.000** on all four models: `hence`,
`indeed`, `namely`, and `whereas` on two of four. A's own word occurs naturally
at 1.3%–10.4%. So the eight are not homogeneous in *how unnatural the forced
production is*: instructing `use however exactly 3 times` asks a model to
amplify something it already does, and `use hence exactly 3 times` asks it to
produce something it never does spontaneously.

This is not a rule violation — §7.6's ceiling is an upper bound and zero is
well inside it — and it is arguably *good* for a companion, since the
instruction's effect is not confounded with a baseline habit. But it is a real
asymmetry between A and its same-form controls, it is not currently recorded
anywhere the reader of an L3 statement would see it, and **it bears directly on
R1**: if a Tier-1 control is harder than A simply because the model never
writes the word, $\Delta_R$ measures that difference and not the pair-specific
mechanism. §13.4's instruction-lift requirement and §13.8's difficulty matching
both operate on $\iota$, which absorbs this — but the mechanism differs, and
the final report should say so.

**Recommendation:** record the natural-rate spread as a permanent limitation
alongside `REFERENCE_POOL_SAME_FORM`, whichever way D4 closes. No rule change.

## 4. Item-by-item

Natural rate = $P(\ge 1 \mid \text{unconstrained})$, worst across the four
models. Ratio = that model's marker rate ÷ **that model's own `however` rate**;
the registered ceiling is absolute (0.15) and the ratio is not a rule, it is the
thing the absolute rule cannot show.

### MK1 `thus` — **APPROVE**
4 letters · consecutive · carried from v4r4's `CX1`
Natural 0.000–0.057; ratio to A 0.00 / 0.08 / 0.73 / **4.40**.
Deterministic, no leakage, shortest marker so the furthest from B's boundary.
The 4.4× ratio on `qwen3-4b-instruct-2507` is an artefact of that model's
unusually low `however` rate (0.013), not of a high `thus` rate (0.057, well
under the 0.15 ceiling). The one marker with prior v5-relevant measurement
history, which is a reason to keep it.

### MK2 `hence` — **APPROVE**
5 letters · consecutive
Natural **0.000 on all four models**. Deterministic, no leakage, no B
interaction. The cleanest item in the vocabulary; see §3 on what zero costs.

### MK3 `though` — **REVIEW**
6 letters · contrastive
Natural 0.003–0.013; ratio 0.05–0.20 — rare, and rarer than A everywhere.
Two substantive concerns, both about **criterion 4**:

1. `though` is the closest single-word English contrastive to `however`. In
   clause-final position (*it works, though*) it is very nearly
   interchangeable with it. §7.6 rule 1 excludes A's own word; it does not
   exclude its nearest synonym, and a reader who holds that a companion
   "counting a word that means what A's word means" is A under another name
   has a case the rules do not answer.
2. **The counter counts two different constructions as one.** `though` is
   both a subordinating conjunction (*though it works, …*) and a concessive
   adverb (*it works, though*). The exact-count instruction does not
   distinguish them, so `use though exactly 3 times` can be satisfied by three
   subordinate clauses, three sentence-final tags, or any mix — three
   different production burdens under one severity. No other registered marker
   has two productive syntactic frames of this kind (`whereas` is
   subordinating only; the rest are adverbial only).

A's declared role is `contrastive` and §13.8 records
`REFERENCE_ROLE_NOT_MATCHED` when no core reference shares it, so removing both
contrastive markers has a cost. `whereas` covers the role. **The judgement is
whether the role match is worth the nearest-synonym objection.**

### MK4 `whereas` — **APPROVE**, with a recorded note
7 letters · contrastive
Natural 0.000–0.013; ratio 0.00–1.00. Deterministic, no leakage.
At the B boundary only at $m = 7$.
**Note for the record:** `whereas` is a subordinating conjunction, so each
forced occurrence requires a two-clause sentence. That is a production burden
of a different *shape* from the adverbial markers — it constrains syntax, not
just lexis — and at high $q$ it interacts with the stem's 60–120 word band
rather than with B. It does not make B easier or harder (criterion 8 is met),
and §13.4's instruction-lift and §12.2's headroom screens both measure the
burden empirically, so this is a note and not an objection.

### MK5 `indeed` — **APPROVE**
6 letters · emphatic
Natural **0.000 on all four models**. Deterministic, no leakage, no B
interaction, a role no other marker occupies.

### MK6 `namely` — **REVIEW**
6 letters · exemplifying
Natural **0.000 on all four models**. Deterministic, no leakage. The marker
itself never violates B.
**Concern — criterion 8, and the one place the researcher's worry actually
bites.** `namely` is an appositive introducer: its function is to be followed
by a *specific* term. In informational prose that term is disproportionately a
long or technical noun — *namely evapotranspiration*, *namely
crystallisation*. So forcing `namely` $q$ times plausibly raises the number of
long words in the answer, which is exactly the mechanical A-like/B interaction
the researcher asked to be checked: the companion would damage B through the
words it drags in, not through the composition mechanism $\Delta_R$ is meant to
isolate.

No other registered marker has this property. `thus`, `hence`, `indeed`,
`instead`, `though` and `overall` are all sentence-adverbial and are followed by
ordinary clauses; `whereas` introduces a clause, not a term.

**This is a hypothesis, not a measurement.** The development evidence cannot
test it: `namely` occurs 0 times in every unconstrained answer, so there is no
natural sample in which to compare word length with and without it, and no
v4r4 cell ever *instructed* it. It is testable prospectively at Gate 1 or on
Calibration-A, at no additional generation cost, by comparing the long-word
share of answers that contain `namely` against those that do not, in the same
solo cell. **Recommendation: measure it before D4 closes, or drop MK6 and
promote a reserve marker.**

### MK7 `instead` — **APPROVE**
7 letters · substitutive
Natural 0.010–0.021; ratio 0.18–0.80. Deterministic, no leakage. At the B
boundary only at $m = 7$. A role no other marker occupies.

### MK8 `overall` — **REVIEW**
7 letters · summative
Natural **0.076–0.112** — the least rare marker by a factor of two, and the
only one whose rate approaches A's own.
Ratio to A: 0.74 / 0.78 / **1.95** / **5.80**. On `qwen3-4b` it is nearly twice
as common as `however`; on `qwen3-4b-instruct-2507` it is nearly six times as
common. It passes the registered rule — 0.112 < 0.15 — because the ceiling was
benchmarked against A's *maximum* rate across models (0.104) rather than
against A on the model in hand.

Three consequences:

1. **Criterion 3 is met formally and is arguably not met substantively.** A
   marker the model already writes in one answer in nine is not a
   "sufficiently rare" instruction target in the sense the criterion intends,
   and the exact-count instruction at low $q$ is partly satisfied by chance.
   §11.2's $\kappa$ correction and §13.5's chance ceiling handle this
   *statistically*; the criterion is about the construct.
2. **`overall` is the one marker with a live alternative spelling.** *over-all*
   is not counted by the verifier (a hyphen splits a word), so a model that
   uses that spelling would be scored as non-compliant while having produced
   the word. Rare in modern usage, deterministic either way, recorded because
   no other marker has the property.
3. It is the marker for which A and the control are most alike on B's own
   lexical feature — both seven letters — which is a point *for* keeping it.

**The judgement is whether the absolute ceiling or the per-model comparison is
the right reading of "sufficiently rare".** If the per-model reading is taken,
MK8 fails on two of four models and a reserve should replace it.

## 6. If a substitution is wanted

§7.10 registers three reserves, admissible **only by a registered amendment**
and only when a registered marker fails Gate 1 — so using one to resolve a
D4 judgement is an amendment, not a substitution within the decision:

| Reserve | Len | Role | Worst natural rate | Would replace |
|---|---:|---|---:|---|
| `notably` | 7 | emphatic | 0.000 | duplicates MK5's role; poor fit for MK3/MK6/MK8 |
| `albeit` | 6 | contrastive | 0.013 | **MK3** — same role, no clause-final frame, not a `however` synonym |
| `besides` | 7 | additive | 0.000 | a role the vocabulary does not currently have; would replace **MK8** and change the role set |

`albeit` is a clean replacement for `though` on both of MK3's concerns. Nothing
in the reserve replaces `namely`'s exemplifying role, so a D4 closure that drops
MK6 loses that role rather than substituting for it.

## 7. The exact remaining researcher choice

Three judgements, none of which the implementation can make:

1. **MK3 `though`** — keep it (accept the near-synonymy, and that the count
   pools two constructions), or amend to `albeit`.
2. **MK6 `namely`** — keep it, measure the long-word hypothesis at Gate 1
   first (recommended: it is free), or drop it and lose the exemplifying role.
3. **MK8 `overall`** — decide whether "sufficiently rare" means the registered
   absolute ceiling of 0.15 or a per-model comparison against A. Under the
   absolute reading MK8 is approved; under the per-model reading it fails on
   two of four models.

Closing D4 at `approve_eight` accepts all three as they stand;
`approve_with_substitution` requires naming which marker moves and to what, and
is a §32 amendment to §7.10's table. `verifiers.constraints.assert_marker_
vocabulary_approved` refuses until D4 is closed and §18 stage 4 calls it before
the regime screen measures a single marker rung — so nothing downstream can run
on an unapproved vocabulary.
